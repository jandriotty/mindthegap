# Project brief — Heat Check-In

## Product

Heat Check-In turns a heat advisory into an explained outreach list—starting with unresolved cooling needs, then missing or outdated plans—and helps behavioral health care teams track each check-in through to its next action.

## Audience

- Operator: social workers, care navigators, and behavioral-health care teams.
- Beneficiaries: New Yorkers with serious mental illness or substance use disorders in an assigned caseload.
- Geography: NYC.
- Initial hazard: heat.

## Main job

Identify whom to contact first, understand why, ask what is still unknown, assign the next action, and verify the outcome.

## Three groups

1. **Unresolved need — contact first.** A support need is known and still open.
2. **Needs verification — check next.** The plan is missing, outdated, or affected by a change.
3. **Plan confirmed — keep visible.** A workable plan is current and no required support need remains open.

Unreachable is a contact outcome, not evidence that a plan is confirmed. A client remains visible for retry or escalation.

## Data principles

- Demo client records are explicitly synthetic.
- Label each relevant field with source and last verification date.
- Separate reported facts, missing information, and neighborhood context.
- A billing date is not a successful contact date.
- Consent/contact permissions are displayed and verified before outreach.
- Demo priority is a workflow rule, not a validated clinical risk prediction.
- Show data completeness and contact recency rather than inventing numeric confidence.
- HVI describes neighborhoods; it does not establish an individual's cooling access.

## Reconciliation of the supplied proposal

The latest elevator pitch uses three operational groups, while earlier notes propose editable weighted risk scores. Recommended MVP: implement the three groups first; defer weighted scores until inputs, meanings, and validation are agreed.

The proposal describes a delivered call sheet, while the visual references show a dashboard. Recommended interpretation: a web call-sheet workspace, with a print-friendly call sheet and supporting map; no actual email/SMS delivery in the initial demo.

These are proposed implementation choices awaiting user feedback, not claims that research has validated them.
